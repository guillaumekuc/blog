Bonjour, 
as forecasted last month, I am now engaged on a project called Vilja.
As a consequence my work style has shifted towards more of a serial style, as opposed to the parallelism I ran previously wherein I was splitting my focus across several projects. This should make for a less diversified newsletter, and a shorter read overall, but the output will hopefully compound faster. 

# CNC Proposal

The proximal target is to produce a proposal/sales document intended for the CNC (Centre National du Cinéma et de l’image animée). The CNC is a French agency which exists to regulate and finance the national media ecosystem. It offers aids by committee, to support artistic diversity and newcomers. Of particular interest to this project at this stage is “L’aide à l’écriture” from the CNC’s FAJV (Fond d’Aide au Jeu Vidéo) which is targeted at authors rather than established companies. The  proclaims a desire for originality, and artistic risk-taking, which is good news. The date for the next deliberation session has already been set: the 21st of September. That is almost 4 months from now. This should be ample time to build a solid presentation deck. As a consequence: most of the month has been spent on conceptual work. Either writing or visual-searching. To me, they are genuinely non-fungible and complementary thinking channels. The images answered questions for me that the writing could not have and vice versa.

# Narrative and concept development

Writing-side: the CNC expects a deck of a dozen pages with several notes in it explaining the narrative, visual, musical, game-mechanic and technical concepts. The copy for the first pages are complete, including the synopsis for the story embedded in the project. This has been an excellent exercise in crystallization and externalization. The project is retrievable on [github](https://github.com/guillaumekuc/vilja-preproduction). 

# Visual development

# Asides

A few asides, since I have space left.

For productivity nerds (sanity nerds) : I stumbled upon the Forster List technique from a Beeminder blogpost. It’s a simple algorithm to run over your todo lists. It leverages structured procrastination energy by ordering tasks in a nice warm-up ramp of psychologically easier tasks leading to the more aversion and difficult ones. It also reduces the cost of planning in the first place by simplifying the process into a series of binary decisions. I find it quite elegant: [Forster Lists: Or Mark Forster's Final Version | Beeminder Blog](https://blog.beeminder.com/forster/)

Also, [Fantastical Calendar](https://flexibits.com/fantastical) has a very nice, clean, soothing UI and is perfect in my setup as a client for a Nextcloud calendar source.
